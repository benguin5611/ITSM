# Style reference

A minimal style guide for formal written documents (business cases, reports, internal memos). Built on widely-accepted plain-English conventions. Adapt to your locale and house style as needed.

---

## Core rules

1. **Sentence case for headings.** `Cost analysis`, not `Cost Analysis` or `COST ANALYSIS`. Proper nouns keep their capitals.
2. **No filler phrases.** Drop "in order to", "it should be noted that", "we'd like to take this opportunity to", "at this point in time".
3. **No hedging when you have a position.** "We recommend X" beats "It may be worth considering X". If you're unsure, say what would resolve the uncertainty.
4. **No trailing punctuation on short bullet items.** Full stops only when a bullet is a complete sentence or contains multiple sentences.
5. **No full stops in acronyms or shortened forms.** `USA`, `KYC`, `Pty Ltd`, `Dr`. Latin abbreviations keep theirs (`i.e.`, `e.g.`).
6. **Numbers under 10 in words, 10 and above as digits.** Exceptions: ages, money, measurements, percentages, and table cells — always digits.
7. **Dates: `13 May 2026` in body text.** Table cells and constrained columns may use `13/05/2026` or `2026-05-13` (ISO). Pick one format per document.
8. **Active voice unless the actor is genuinely unknown.** "The board approved the budget" beats "The budget was approved".
9. **Direct address.** Use "you" for the reader and "we" for the authoring team, unless your house style forbids it.
10. **No contractions in formal documents.** `do not`, not `don't`. Customer-facing comms and blog posts are different — contractions are usually fine there.

---

## Locale notes

Pick one spelling convention (US, UK, Australian) and apply consistently. Common Australian/UK divergences from US:

- `-ise` / `-isation` (not `-ize` / `-ization`): `organise`, `realisation`
- `-our` (not `-or`): `colour`, `behaviour`, `favour`
- `-re` (not `-er`): `centre`, `metre`
- `program` is acceptable in both (technical and general use)
- `judgement` (UK/AU) vs `judgment` (US, and UK legal)

---

## Lists and bullets

- Parallel structure: every bullet starts the same way (all noun phrases, or all verb phrases, or all full sentences).
- Don't introduce a list with a colon if the sentence runs naturally into it (`The team includes Sarah, John, and Mei.` — no colon).
- Use a colon when the introduction is a complete clause and the list completes it.

---

## Voice and tone for formal documents

- Direct, clear, respectful of the reader's time.
- No marketing language ("leveraging", "synergies", "best-in-class", "world-class").
- No editorialising adverbs ("clearly", "obviously", "simply"). If it were obvious, you wouldn't be writing it down.
- State the recommendation before the rationale. Executives skim — front-load the decision.

---

## Quick decision tree

1. Is this a heading? → Sentence case.
2. Is this a complete sentence? → End with a full stop. Otherwise, no trailing punctuation.
3. Is this an acronym? → No full stops between letters.
4. Is this a formal document? → No contractions.
5. Is the spelling consistent with the rest of the document? → If not, fix it.
