# Security awareness and training standard

> Part of the [docs-kit](../README.md#before-you-rely-on-anything-here): a Department-of-One starting point, rather than a certified or audit-ready control out of the box. Read that disclaimer and this artefact's own "Adapt this to your context" section before relying on it.

## Introduction

### Purpose

This standard defines how the organisation builds and maintains staff knowledge of information security practices, so that people, alongside technical controls, are equipped to prevent, recognise, and report security incidents. It covers baseline awareness training for all staff as well as role-specific training for higher-risk functions.

### Risks

- Increased likelihood of successful social engineering or phishing attacks
- Security incidents caused by avoidable human error
- Failure to meet customer, partner, or regulatory training expectations
- Slower incident detection and reporting
- Reduced overall security culture and accountability

## Requirements

**Responsible party must:**

| Ref | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| SAT-100 | Provide role-specific training to personnel with security incident response responsibilities. |
| SAT-101 | Periodically assess where staff security knowledge or behaviour needs improvement, and use the result to guide training priorities. |
| SAT-102 | Deliver training that addresses the gaps identified through that assessment. |
| SAT-103 | Run a baseline security-awareness program for all staff at least annually. |
| SAT-104 | Update the security-awareness program at least annually to reflect new threats, technologies, and business needs. |
| SAT-105 | Train staff on secure authentication practices, including choosing strong credentials, protecting them, and not reusing passwords across accounts. |
| SAT-106 | Train staff to recognise common social engineering techniques, such as phishing, pretexting, and impersonation. |
| SAT-107 | Train staff to correctly classify, store, transfer, archive, and dispose of sensitive information in line with the organisation's data handling expectations. |
| SAT-108 | Train staff on common causes of accidental data exposure, such as lost devices or misaddressed emails. |
| SAT-109 | Train staff to recognise common indicators of a security incident and know how to report one. |
| SAT-110 | Give new joiners the security information and support they need to understand their responsibilities from day one. |
| SAT-111 | Provide secure coding training to staff with software development responsibilities. |
| SAT-112 | Maintain a record of security training completion for all personnel, including what was completed, when, and the outcome where assessed. |

## Guidelines

**Responsible party should/may:**

| Ref | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| SAT-200 | Run simulated phishing exercises periodically to measure the real-world effectiveness of security-awareness training. |
| SAT-201 | Define and track metrics on training completion and effectiveness, and report them to leadership. |
| SAT-202 | Encourage attendance at relevant external training, conferences, or workshops to supplement internal training. |
| SAT-203 | Encourage staff to actively promote and take part in security initiatives across the organisation. |

## Adapt this to your context

- A solo or very small team can combine the skills-gap assessment and training delivery into one lightweight annual review rather than running them as separate programs.
- Scale phishing-simulation frequency and secure-coding training depth to the size of your engineering team and the sensitivity of the data you handle.
- If you're working towards a compliance framework, confirm what training evidence it expects and adjust the record-keeping requirement to match, rather than over- or under-collecting.
- New-joiner security training can be a short checklist and conversation in a very small team, provided it still gets recorded.

**Frameworks referenced**: ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A, NIST Cybersecurity Framework
