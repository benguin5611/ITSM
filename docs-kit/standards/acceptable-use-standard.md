# Acceptable use standard

> Part of the [docs-kit](../README.md#before-you-rely-on-anything-here): a Department-of-One starting point, rather than a certified or audit-ready control out of the box. Read that disclaimer and this artefact's own "Adapt this to your context" section before relying on it.

## Introduction

### Purpose

This standard sets expectations for how staff use company-provided internet access, devices, accounts, and network resources, covering both business and incidental personal use. It exists to keep everyday computer and internet use secure and productive without needing to spell out every possible scenario in advance.

### Risks

- Security incidents caused by unsafe browsing, downloads, or account-sharing
- Introduction of malware onto company systems or networks
- Legal or regulatory exposure from inappropriate or non-compliant online activity
- Lost productivity from excessive personal use during work hours
- Reputational damage from behaviour associated with the organisation online

## Requirements

**Responsible party must:**

| Ref | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| AUP-100 | Use company internet access and network resources primarily for business-related purposes. |
| AUP-101 | Use professional, respectful language in any online communication made on behalf of the organisation. |
| AUP-102 | Ensure information shared or accessed online complies with company policy and applicable legal or regulatory obligations. |
| AUP-103 | Avoid websites, services, or downloads that could compromise the security or integrity of company systems or data. |
| AUP-104 | Follow the organisation's guidance when using social media for business promotion or communication. |
| AUP-105 | Use company-provided communication tools, not personal accounts, for business correspondence. |
| AUP-106 | Take care when sending messages containing sensitive information, to avoid accidental disclosure to the wrong recipient. |
| AUP-107 | Disable or avoid auto-forwarding rules that risk sending sensitive information to unauthorised recipients. |
| AUP-108 | Keep personal internet, email, and messaging use during work hours to a level that does not interfere with work responsibilities. |
| AUP-109 | Not access illegal, offensive, or inappropriate content using company systems or networks. |
| AUP-110 | Use strong, unique credentials for company accounts and never share credentials with anyone else. |
| AUP-111 | Not attempt to bypass, disable, or circumvent security controls or web filtering put in place by the organisation. |
| AUP-112 | Understand that reasonable monitoring of network and internet usage may occur to detect security threats and confirm compliance with this standard. |
| AUP-113 | Report suspected violations of this standard, or observed security incidents, promptly to IT or the designated security contact. |
| AUP-114 | Not use company systems or network resources for illegal file sharing, distributing malicious software, unauthorised financial transactions or gambling, cryptocurrency mining, or harassing or discriminatory behaviour. |

## Guidelines

**Responsible party should/may:**

| Ref | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| AUP-200 | If in doubt about acceptable personal use, ask IT or the designated security contact for guidance. |
| AUP-201 | Share files via links rather than attachments to avoid unnecessary mailbox or storage congestion. |
| AUP-202 | Keep mailbox and storage use within reasonable limits, using archiving tools where available. |
| AUP-203 | Exercise extra caution on public or untrusted Wi-Fi, and avoid accessing sensitive company systems or making financial transactions over such networks. |
| AUP-204 | Request specific websites be unblocked through an established process rather than attempting to bypass filtering. |

## Adapt this to your context

- Decide explicitly where the line sits on personal use (some organisations are generous, others restrictive), and make sure the position you document is one you're actually willing to enforce.
- If you don't operate web filtering, content monitoring, or DLP tooling, adjust or remove the clauses that assume they exist rather than leaving requirements nobody is checking.
- Extend equivalent expectations to contractors and third parties with system access, even if delivered through a contract rather than this standard directly.
- Revisit the prohibited-use list periodically as new categories of risk emerge, such as unsanctioned AI tools or personal cloud storage.
