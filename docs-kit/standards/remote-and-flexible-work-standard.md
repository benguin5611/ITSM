# Remote and flexible work standard

> Part of the [docs-kit](../README.md#before-you-rely-on-anything-here): a Department-of-One starting point, rather than a certified or audit-ready control out of the box. Read that disclaimer and this artefact's own "Adapt this to your context" section before relying on it.

## Introduction

### Purpose

This standard sets out the information-security and operational expectations for staff working remotely or on a flexible schedule, so that flexibility in when and where people work doesn't come at the cost of protecting company data, systems, and equipment. It applies in addition to any separate people/HR policy covering leave, entitlements, or employment terms, rather than replacing it.

### Risks

- Data breaches or unauthorised access via poorly secured home or remote networks
- Loss, theft, or damage to company equipment outside the office
- Reduced ability to detect and respond to incidents originating from remote endpoints
- Workplace injury with unclear accountability
- Inconsistent application of flexible-work arrangements creating friction or unfairness
- Operational disruption if remote access isn't planned for

## Requirements

**Responsible party must:**

| Ref | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| RFW-100 | Agree on a documented remote-work arrangement before working remotely on an ongoing basis, rather than on an ad hoc basis. |
| RFW-101 | Access company networks and systems only from organisation-approved devices, unless a documented exception has been formally approved. |
| RFW-102 | Connect to company resources only through an approved VPN or equivalent zero-trust remote access solution that encrypts traffic in transit. |
| RFW-103 | Maintain a secure physical workspace that protects company equipment and any sensitive information from unauthorised access, loss, or theft. |
| RFW-104 | Meet a minimum standard of internet connectivity sufficient for reliable, uninterrupted access to company systems. |
| RFW-105 | Report any suspected security incident or data breach connected to a remote-working arrangement to IT or the designated security contact promptly. |
| RFW-106 | Cooperate fully with any incident response activity related to a remote-working arrangement, including preserving relevant evidence when asked. |
| RFW-107 | Return all company-provided equipment when a remote-work arrangement ends or on request. |
| RFW-108 | Agree with their manager on a small number of regular work locations, and keep work performed outside those locations to a minimum. |
| RFW-109 | Obtain prior written approval before working from outside their normal country of residence, given the legal, tax, and security implications. |
| RFW-110 | Take reasonable care for their own health and safety, and that of anyone else present, while working in a remote location. |
| RFW-111 | Keep the designated remote workspace free of hazards and notify the organisation promptly of any change that creates a health, safety, or security risk. |
| RFW-112 | Notify their manager promptly of any injury sustained in the designated workspace during work hours. |

## Guidelines

**Responsible party should/may:**

| Ref | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| RFW-200 | Consider requests for a more permanent flexible-work arrangement (different hours, location, or schedule) on their individual merits, weighed against genuine business needs. |
| RFW-201 | Put any agreed change to a flexible or remote-working arrangement in writing, describing what is changing and why, before it takes effect. |
| RFW-202 | Review flexible and remote-working arrangements periodically to confirm they continue to work for both the individual and the organisation. |
| RFW-203 | Maintain regular contact with the team through agreed channels while working flexibly or remotely, so availability stays predictable. |
| RFW-204 | Build in some in-person collaboration time where practical, even in a predominantly remote or flexible setup. |
| RFW-205 | Apply extra physical security measures (such as locking devices away or using a privacy screen) where the sensitivity of the work justifies it. |

## Adapt this to your context

- This standard focuses on the information-security side of remote work; broader employment matters (leave, entitlements, statutory flexible-work requests) vary by jurisdiction and belong in an HR policy backed by local legal advice.
- Scale the equipment and connectivity requirements to your risk profile. A two-person startup can reasonably run a lighter-touch approach than a business handling regulated customer data.
- If most of the team works remotely by default, consider whether this belongs as a joint IT/People-and-Culture document rather than an IT-only standard.
- Revisit approved work locations and any cross-border work approvals whenever someone's circumstances change.
